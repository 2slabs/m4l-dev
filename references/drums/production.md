# Lo-fi / sleepy dub techno drum production: sourced notes

Style anchor: Deepchord (Rod Modell), Echospace (Modell + Stephen Hitchell), cv313 / Soultek / Intrusion (Hitchell); Fluxion, bvdub haze. NOT Basic Channel / Maurizio punch.
Compiled 2026-10-15 from fetched page text (curl or WebFetch). Every bullet ends with its URL.
Source quality tags: [INTERVIEW] artist's own words; [PRESS] third-party review or label blurb (descriptive only, not production fact); [TUTORIAL] magazine tutorial (a DAW recipe, NOT what the artists did); [FETCH-EXTRACT] quote relayed by the WebFetch summariser (not a raw page read); [ANECDOTAL] forum or comment.
Hard finding: Modell says he refuses to discuss gear. No source gives the artists' drum machine models, patterns, BPMs or hat filter settings (see Gaps).

## Deepchord / Echospace

- [INTERVIEW] Modell: "I always start with the ambient parts when making a song. I never start with a kick drum or bass line. Those are just dropped in last minute and are the least important—they are just a metronome for the ambient elements." https://www.vice.com/en/article/get-to-know-deepchords-rod-modell-the-detroit-artist-who-still-wants-to-loop-till-infinity/
- [INTERVIEW] Modell, same article: tracks "start out as 45-60 minute pieces that get edited for the album"; loops are left playing "for hours or days"; "I view each song as a little sound sculpture that can be looped forever at low volume." https://www.vice.com/en/article/get-to-know-deepchords-rod-modell-the-detroit-artist-who-still-wants-to-loop-till-infinity/
- [INTERVIEW] Modell: "Psychoacoustic experiments set to a kick drum"; "lots of processed field recordings swirling around in the background and little sonic occurrences that the listener doesn't realize they are hearing until after they're gone." https://www.vice.com/en/article/get-to-know-deepchords-rod-modell-the-detroit-artist-who-still-wants-to-loop-till-infinity/
- [INTERVIEW] Modell on gear talk: "I don't speak much about this because I truly and sincerely think it makes no difference"; "You can make a great record with a used $150 synth and some cheap Electro Harmonix pedals." https://www.vice.com/en/article/get-to-know-deepchords-rod-modell-the-detroit-artist-who-still-wants-to-loop-till-infinity/
- [INTERVIEW] Modell on Basic Channel: "It kind of does bother me, because it's not really true"; bigger influences named: "Wolfgang Voigt's sample-based soundscapes are probably 3X the influence", 1990s ambient, Steve Roach, Ashra, Lustmord; "I think Bandulu taught me more about incorporating dub elements than BC did." https://www.vice.com/en/article/get-to-know-deepchords-rod-modell-the-detroit-artist-who-still-wants-to-loop-till-infinity/
- [INTERVIEW] Modell, 2015: "I only quantize percussion and bass, and use loopers for everything else." Loopers named: Looperlative LP-1, Lexicon JamMan. https://headphonecommute.com/2015/12/29/in-the-studio-with-rod-modell/
- [INTERVIEW] Modell, 2015: "I do enjoy playing with cheap samplers. Anything that you drop into them sounds completely different when you play the sample back. They're especially nice when you combine them with external analog filters and FX. Synths just can't match their beautiful, grainy sound." https://headphonecommute.com/2015/12/29/in-the-studio-with-rod-modell/
- [INTERVIEW] Modell, 2015: "I mostly use samples that I made of hardware that I no longer use"; room contents "a mixing console, computer, and stack or 3-4 old samplers"; still has "an Eventide Orville and Lexicon PCM-80"; prefers "an Akai s900"; first drum machine mentioned: "a brand new, factory sealed TR-808" bought in the early 1980s. https://headphonecommute.com/2015/12/29/in-the-studio-with-rod-modell/
- [INTERVIEW] Modell, 2015: analog tape was "a 2500' reel" at "30 ips" for $70; replaced by Steinberg Nuendo ("Software recorders just replaced an analog reel-to-reel for me"). https://headphonecommute.com/2015/12/29/in-the-studio-with-rod-modell/
- [INTERVIEW] Modell, 2015: "I like distressing sound. Stretching things to the breaking point"; "The kick drum and bass are incidental and least important." https://headphonecommute.com/2015/12/29/in-the-studio-with-rod-modell/
- [INTERVIEW] Modell, 2015: starts with a drone or pad, "Then add some kind or pulse to it"; "If it still sounds ok 6 hours later, I'll try to fit in a kick or bassline." He auditions "versions without the bassline or kick drum." https://headphonecommute.com/2015/12/29/in-the-studio-with-rod-modell/
- [INTERVIEW] Modell, 2014: "I was sequencing with an old Akai ASQ-10, Linn 9000 or Studio 440" (around 2007, no laptop); "I'm into vintage FX processors more than synths"; owns two Prophet VS. https://www.vice.com/en/article/gv3zn3/deepchord-interview-unsound-brooklyn
- [INTERVIEW] Modell, 2014: field-recording rigs: Crown S.A.S.S. mic + Tascam DA-P1 DAT (mid-1990s), DPA 4060, Pearl MS2CL, Sennheiser MKH418, Soundman OKM II, Sony PCM-D50 ("cheap recorders with built-in mics"). https://www.vice.com/en/article/gv3zn3/deepchord-interview-unsound-brooklyn

- [PRESS] Label-page copy (echospace313.com): Echospace "Eschewing the dry, metallic scrapings and sound design-intensive ideas that dominate Berlin... lush emotionalism, hazed out into soft focus with monumental amounts of dub effects." https://echospace313.com/deepchord
- [PRESS] Label-page copy (echospace313.com), cv313: "storming waves of driving beats over a milky smooth ambience"; "shuffling rhythms, blunted beats"; "pin-sharp hi-hats" on "Irradiate". https://echospace313.com/deepchord

- [INTERVIEW] Fluxion (CDM): "As a general rule, I never narrow the space in productions. Early kit: SH-101, JX-3P, "a Sequential Circuits drum machine, if I remember correctly". https://cdm.link/fluxion-interview-haze/

## Kicks

- [TUTORIAL] Attack "Basic Channel-style" beat: sample chosen for "a good thumpy transient as well as some subby low end that lacks sustain" because a separate sub bass is added later. https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/basic-channel-style-dub-techno/
- [TUTORIAL] Attack: Drum Buss on the whole drum rack, "Boom setting at 8% at around 37Hz and a Damp setting of around 8.5kHz". https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/basic-channel-style-dub-techno/
- [TUTORIAL] Attack: sub bass sidechained to kick; EQ after the drum rack "to reduce the frequencies below around 37Hz and use a limiter". https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/basic-channel-style-dub-techno/
- [TUTORIAL] Attack synth-chords piece ends with "a muffled 909 kick and sub bass" (no numbers for the kick). https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/dub-techno-synth-chords/
- [TUTORIAL] Attack techno kick (not dub-specific, loud-kick recipe): Glue Compressor "about -5dB of gain reduction"; cut "around 120Hz" and "around 800Hz"; low shelf boost "around 80Hz". Distortion "softens the transients, whilst simultaneously adding volume". https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/tutorials/adding-reverb-techno-kick/
- [INTERVIEW] Muffled / buried kick, artist side: Modell auditions with and without kick and calls it a "metronome" (see Deepchord section). Press says "muffled kick drums" (Textura, above). No artist gives a kick Hz or dB figure.

## Hats

- [TUTORIAL] Attack: open hat on the off-beat; "Use an EQ to cut the unnecessary low frequencies while boosting the top end", so the airy part cuts through; kick at 145 BPM with swing none. https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/basic-channel-style-dub-techno/
- [TUTORIAL] Attack Dub Machines (Modnetic) on an 8th-note closed hat at 140 BPM: Repeat "303ms", middle tape head only, Wow "about 9 o'clock", Intensity (feedback) "35%", Level "about +7dB", Character "Old", "extreme Noise settings"; reverb "Eurorack". Limiter advised at higher feedback. https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/tutorials/dub-style-drum-processing-for-character-and-groove/
- [TUTORIAL] Attack Dub Machines on a crash: Repeat "2/16T" (triplet sixteenth), single head, feedback "85%", Character "Lo-fi". https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/tutorials/dub-style-drum-processing-for-character-and-groove/
- [TUTORIAL] MusicTech dub delay chain test material: "high hats and snare hits sequenced at 74 bpm". https://musictech.com/tutorials/how-to-create-dub-delay/
- [PRESS] Descriptions only: "hi-hats lisp" (Incense and Black Light), "backbeat hi-hat tics" (Coldest Season Vol. 4), "double-time" hats (Intrusion). https://echospace313.com/deepchord ; https://www.textura.org/archives/d/deepchord4_cd.htm ; http://www.textura.org/archives/d/deepchord_intrusion.htm
- DISAGREEMENT on hat EQ: Attack boosts hat top end after cutting lows; the user's brief wants low-passed/dusty hats; no fetched source gives a hat low-pass cutoff. See Gaps.

## Rim/clap/percussion into delay

- [TUTORIAL] Attack Basic Channel beat: snare is "808-style" on every other downbeat; clap doubles it, pitched up, Redux, EQ cuts lows "until about 150Hz". https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/basic-channel-style-dub-techno/
- [TUTORIAL] Attack: percussion loop extracted from a 145 BPM sample; hits placed "exactly on offbeat 16th notes to keep everything quantized"; "one of the hallmarks of dub techno is groove and movement resulting from delay instead of heavily swung drum parts." https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/basic-channel-style-dub-techno/
- [TUTORIAL] Attack: percussion chain = Amp (distortion) + Echo "1/8 dotted" + Erosion (Wide Noise) + Drum Buss (Transients up) + EQ + sidechain compressor from kick; second layer adds Live Delay "5/16 echoes". https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/basic-channel-style-dub-techno/
- [TUTORIAL] Attack Dub Machines rimshot: Diffuse repeat "7/32" beats, Regen "100%", Size "just past halfway", Diffuse "around 25%", "Gallop" pattern, Rectify up a little, Width full. Snare: spring reverb, Phaser Rate "about 30%", Amount "10%", Mix "50/50", Bass down, Treble up, Noise up, Input up to saturate. https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/tutorials/dub-style-drum-processing-for-character-and-groove/
- [TUTORIAL] Attack dub chords, delay returns: Echo "1/8 dotted" (chords step in the drum article); dual-tap tape delay Tap 1 "1/8 Dotted", feedback "around 20%"; Tap 2 free-running "264ms", feedback "85% or so"; send "100%"; send Auto Filter high-pass shelf "280Hz", LFO amount 22, rate 0.25 Hz. https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/dub-techno-synth-chords/
- [TUTORIAL] Attack dub chords EQ: "reduce the mids at 600Hz by around -7dB with a wide Q", low-pass above 4.5 kHz, high-pass below 100 Hz. https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/synth-secrets/dub-techno-synth-chords/
- [TUTORIAL] MusicTech dub delay: sync repeats to "⅛ or ¼ notes" but nudge them "slightly out of sync" (analog tape had no sync); chain = peak compressor (high ratio, low threshold), delay, low-pass ("poor high frequency response of analog tape"), chorus for warble, RMS compressor (fast attack, slow release); automate send as a "delay throw". https://musictech.com/tutorials/how-to-create-dub-delay/

## Saturation / bit reduction / filtering

- [TUTORIAL] Attack: Redux "Bit Reduction all the way down to six" on the chord sample; bandpass Auto Filter, slope 12, cutoff "around 450", LFO amount "around 10". https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/basic-channel-style-dub-techno/
- [TUTORIAL] Attack Dub Machines parallel tape saturation: Modnetic on a send at Mix 100%, send "almost halfway", Character "501" turned fully right, Bass boosted, Treble reduced "to mimic the EQ curve of tape", small Noise. https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/tutorials/dub-style-drum-processing-for-character-and-groove/
- [INTERVIEW] Artist-side saturation: grainy cheap samplers into external analog filters and FX (Modell, headphonecommute above); "12-bit sampling equipment" (Hitchell, Textura, FETCH-EXTRACT). No bit depth or filter value given for the drums themselves. https://headphonecommute.com/2015/12/29/in-the-studio-with-rod-modell/ ; https://www.textura.org/archives/interviews/tenquestions_deepchord_soultek.htm
- [TUTORIAL] Attack distortion note: distortion "softens the transients, whilst simultaneously adding volume". https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/tutorials/adding-reverb-techno-kick/

## Sidechain

- [TUTORIAL] Attack: Live Compressor, sidechain input = kick, "attack to its fastest setting", "reduce the threshold to taste"; the same compressor copied onto chords, percussion and both sub-bass hits. No ratio or release given. https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/basic-channel-style-dub-techno/
- [TUTORIAL] Attack techno kick: reverb return sidechained by the kick group gives "the classic pumping effect" and "more space in the mix" (no numbers). https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/tutorials/adding-reverb-techno-kick/

## Where sources disagree

- Gear importance: Modell says gear "makes no difference" (Vice) and "I'm not dependent on any gear", yet he and Hitchell list specific hardware (Space Echo, samplers) elsewhere. Treat the lists as the toolbox, not the sound. https://www.vice.com/en/article/get-to-know-deepchords-rod-modell-the-detroit-artist-who-still-wants-to-loop-till-infinity/ ; https://www.textura.org/archives/d/deepchord4_cd.htm
- Hardware vs computer: Modell 2014 says hardware was never a "conscientious decision"; Modell 2015 says "I do have a recent fascination with vintage samplers, but that's really about it for hardware" and "I could never return to all hardware"; Hitchell says Coldest Season (2007) had no computer. Era matters. https://www.vice.com/en/article/gv3zn3/deepchord-interview-unsound-brooklyn ; https://headphonecommute.com/2015/12/29/in-the-studio-with-rod-modell/ ; https://www.gridface.com/stephen-hitchell-interview/
- Kick importance vs press: Modell calls the kick "least important" but press (Textura, Igloo) describes "pounding" / "throbbing" kicks and "massive thrust". Keep the kick low and soft in the mix but present. https://headphonecommute.com/2015/12/29/in-the-studio-with-rod-modell/ ; http://www.textura.org/archives/d/deepchord_intrusion.htm
- Basic Channel lineage: press repeatedly compares; Modell denies it. Attack's recipe is a Basic Channel one, so its numbers are a proxy, not Deepchord. https://www.vice.com/en/article/get-to-know-deepchords-rod-modell-the-detroit-artist-who-still-wants-to-loop-till-infinity/
- Hat voicing: Attack boosts hat highs; the dub-delay school low-passes the delay return (MusicTech); no source low-passes the dry hat. https://www.attackmagazine.com/technique/beat-dissected/basic-channel-style-dub-techno/ ; https://musictech.com/tutorials/how-to-create-dub-delay/
