# TR-909 voice generation: sourced facts (for gen~ M4L drum device)

Compiled 2026-10-15. Every bullet ends with the URL it was read from; local copies in `mirror/` (see Mirrored files). Numbers appear only if seen in fetched text.
URL shorthand is NOT used: bullets carry full URLs.
Primary = Roland service notes OCR (scan text layer from archive.org; schematics themselves are images and were NOT read; the PDF copies have no text layer, `pdftotext` returns ~16 bytes). OCR is noisy; garbled spots are flagged.
Secondary = Sound On Sound "Synth Secrets" (Gordon Reid), network-909.de circuit analysis (annotates the service-manual schematics), Robin Whittle mod manual (cites real part refs from the service-manual schematic), TipTop manual, Attack Magazine interview.

## Architecture (whole machine)
- Voices split into Analog (BD, SD, LT/MT/HT, RS, CP) and Digital (CH, OH, Crash, Ride). CPU gives both kinds TRIG and ACCENT data identically. https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt
- TRIG: latched, appears as a 5 V positive pulse for 2 ms on the voice's pin; used alone or with ACCENT to reset generators and start envelopes (pitch, tone colour, contour, loudness). https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt
- ACCENT: latches (IC2-IC9) -> resistor array (RM0621) -> analog voltage held (clamped) until replaced by next data. https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt
- Analog voices (SD, BD, toms) share one noise generator (network-909 adds hand clap to the sharers). https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt , http://www.network-909.de/handclap.htm
- Noise generator: digital pseudo-random, two shift registers in cascade (IC32, IC33) clocked by two Ex-OR gates of IC31 "at a higher frequency" for more HF content; triggered once at power-up via D48. https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt
- Noise is a repeating digital waveform, 0 to 15 V logic levels, unlike the TR-808's noisy transistor. https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- Design intent: Roland aimed for a "drum synthesizer" with analog sources so tone could be changed in real time with knobs; cymbals only went digital because analog cymbals failed to satisfy and the deadline loomed. https://www.attackmagazine.com/features/interview/atsushi-hoshiai-the-man-behind-the-tr-909/
- Every voice has a Level knob (volume). Rim shot and clap have only Level in the owner's manual sound section; no Tune/Decay for them. https://archive.org/download/synthmanual-roland-tr-909-owners-manual/rolandtr-909ownersmanual_djvu.txt
- Trigger out (rim shot): +14 V, 20 ms pulse. Master out 6 Vp-p, 1 kohm. https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt

## Accent, Flam, Shuffle (sequencer-side)
- Two accent types: Accent A "Total Accent" (set per step, applies to all voices in the step, intensity set by the Total Accent knob) and Accent B (per voice per step; BD, SD, LT, MT, HT, Closed HH only). https://archive.org/download/synthmanual-roland-tr-909-owners-manual/rolandtr-909ownersmanual_djvu.txt
- Accent B level cannot be changed (fixed); both can be set at once, but with Total Accent knob high, Accent B may be unnoticeable. https://archive.org/download/synthmanual-roland-tr-909-owners-manual/rolandtr-909ownersmanual_djvu.txt
- Step write: button off / dim = normal volume / bright = accent. Open HH = Closed-HH normal+accent keys (13 and 14) pressed together. https://archive.org/download/synthmanual-roland-tr-909-owners-manual/rolandtr-909ownersmanual_djvu.txt
- Circuit effect of accent (BD): accent changes amplitude of ENV1's attack and its decay rate (SOS BD diagram); in toms, accent hits envelope circuit and the noise VCA: louder plus "slightly more attack and noise" (TipTop clone manual, describing behaviour as modelled on the 909). https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/practical-bass-drum-synthesis , https://www.tiptopaudio.com/manuals/Tiptop_Audio_TOMS909_ns.pdf
- Circuit effect of accent (SD): accent voltage sets the start voltage of the VCO1/VCO2 amplitude-envelope capacitors (C70, C79): higher accent starts higher and lasts longer; ENV4 (main snappy noise) start voltage set by accent via D60; ENV5 (HF noise) is the same regardless of accent; the pitch pulse ENV1 (C56) is the same for all notes. https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf  (service notes contradict the ENV5 part, see Gaps)
- Flam: writable on BD, SD, LT, MT, HT only; 8 interval options chosen with Main Keys 9-16 (longer toward 16) while holding Shuffle/Flam; first hit is on the step, second follows; interval does NOT depend on Tempo; set only during step write; new data replaces old (no dubbing flam over non-flam). https://archive.org/download/synthmanual-roland-tr-909-owners-manual/rolandtr-909ownersmanual_djvu.txt
- Shuffle: only when scale is one of the two shuffle-capable scales (note symbols lost in OCR); bounce set with Main Keys 1-7 holding Shuffle/Flam; one scale has 7 options (1 = no effect, faster bounce toward 7), the other has 4 options (1=2 none, 3=4, 5=6, 7); with odd number of measures the last step takes no bounce. https://archive.org/download/synthmanual-roland-tr-909-owners-manual/rolandtr-909ownersmanual_djvu.txt

## BD (bass drum)
- Owner's manual knobs: Level; Attack ("controls the attack sound", clockwise increases); Decay (clockwise longer, fully CCW mutes); Tune ("controls the pitch"; whatever Tune is, Decay still acts at its set time). https://archive.org/download/synthmanual-roland-tr-909-owners-manual/rolandtr-909ownersmanual_djvu.txt
- Two parts: (1) tonal VCO body, (2) click/attack = pulse + low-passed noise. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/practical-bass-drum-synthesis , http://www.network-909.de/bassdrum.htm
- VCO: triangle wave (integrator VCO, same design as snare) shaped by diode clipper to approximate sine. https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf , http://www.network-909.de/bassdrum.htm  (SOS says sawtooth + waveshaper: see Gaps)
- Clipper: two back-to-back diodes conducting about 0.5-0.6 V above/below ground; driving them harder gives a squarer wave. https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- VCO reset: TRIG also discharges C14 via Q11 so the VCO restarts at every trigger. http://www.network-909.de/bassdrum.htm
- Pitch envelope: ENV-3 started by TRIG drives the Control Voltage Generator, i.e. the oscillator starts higher and sweeps down to base frequency; SOS: pitch EG has instant attack and slow decay. https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf , http://www.network-909.de/bassdrum.htm , https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/practical-bass-drum-synthesis
- TUNE knob in the circuit: Whittle: Tune does not set the base oscillator frequency; it sets the decay time of the pitch-sweep envelope (start high, sweep to normal). network-909: VR2 (TUNE) adjusts modulation depth of the VCO pitch modulation. https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf , http://www.network-909.de/bassdrum.htm  (disagree with each other and with the owner's manual: Gaps)
- Sweep depth is set by R28 (150k) in the original; replacing with 300k gives about 2x sweep; a 330k gives 2.2x. Mod pot "Pitch" range about 0.43x to 4.7x of normal base frequency (base frequency is not user-adjustable on the stock unit). https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- Amplitude: VCA Q12 follows ENV-1, whose shape "looks like a compressed drum sound" (cause of the punch); ENV-1 is driven by ACCENT through Q4. https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf (decay x4 mod), http://www.network-909.de/bassdrum.htm
- Click path: pulse generator = low-pass (Q8, R5, R6, R14, C22) + band-pass (Q9, C2, C3, C4, R7, R8, R15, R16) shaping the trigger into a pulse; mixed with low-passed common noise; VCA Q6 under ENV-2; then mixed with VCO path at IC11a with level control. http://www.network-909.de/bassdrum.htm
- ATTACK knob = amount of click (EG2 level) is the SOS description of the Analogue Solutions BD99 clone's added control, inferred (not stated) to be the 909 Attack knob. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/practical-bass-drum-synthesis
- Whittle: stock max decay is about 1/4 of the modded maximum (mod extends 4x); no absolute ms shown in text. https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- Designer: Mr. Kikumoto designed the kick sound; Mr. Oe the analog sound sources. https://www.attackmagazine.com/features/interview/atsushi-hoshiai-the-man-behind-the-tr-909/

## SD (snare)
- Owner's manual knobs: Level; Tone ("change timbre; clockwise brighter"); Tune (pitch of the snare); Snappy ("snappiness"; fully CCW = snappy removed). https://archive.org/download/synthmanual-roland-tr-909-owners-manual/rolandtr-909ownersmanual_djvu.txt
- Four elements: VCO1 + VCA, VCO2 + VCA (the "Drum"), and two noise paths (the "Snappy"). https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt , https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- Oscillators: two triangle VCOs (hysteresis comparator IC37a, voltage-dependent-resistor inverter buffers in IC36, integrator IC37b with C69 and Q44 reset switch); VCO1 lower frequency (different capacitors C69 vs C71); VCO2 "tuned somewhat higher". https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt , https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- Triangle rounded to approximate sine by back-to-back diodes (D65/D66 for VCO1). https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf , http://www.network-909.de/snaredru.htm
- On TRIG: VCO1 comparator forced low, C69 discharged (VCO restarts at known phase so VCO2 starts in phase), noise-path VCA Q50 muted during reset. https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt
- Pitch bend: control voltage generator IC35 supplies buffer rails proportional to ENV-1; charging rate of C69 changes "for about 20 ms" = pitch bend of the snare for that period. Pitch CV = Tune pot voltage + a short rising/falling pulse (ENV1 on C56). https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt , https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- Same pitch CV drives both VCOs. Tune pot affects both VCO frequencies and has no effect on Snappy. https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- Amplitude: each VCO has its own single-transistor VCA (Q50, Q51) following its own envelope (ENV3 / ENV2); VCO outputs are always fully mixed into the output (only Tune, Level, Snappy and Tone act around them). https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt , https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- Noise path: common noise -> low-pass filter (IC40a) -> split: (a) VCA Q48 under ENV4 (Whittle: path has a high-pass stage with C80 = 0.022 uF); (b) high-pass (IC39a) -> VCA Q47 under ENV5; recombined at IC39b, then through the Snappy pot (volume control for both noise components), then summed with the drum and Level amp IC40b. http://www.network-909.de/snaredru.htm , https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- SOS reading of same block diagram: LPF then two paths: [HPF -> VCA4/CG5] and [VCA3/CG4], mixer, another VCA, final mixer. Gives different high/low-band amplitude and decay. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/practical-snare-drum-synthesis
- Tone knob in the circuit (Whittle): VR7 (500k) with R254 (100k) sets the discharge, i.e. the decay of ENV4, the main snappy component; so "Tone" is the noise decay time (brighter/longer). https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- ENV4 stock shape: holds at max for about 24 ms (C68) before decaying; decays toward a slightly negative voltage (faster than exponential); VCA Q48 passes nothing below about 0.4 V control. https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- SOS: Roland ignored shifted harmonic series, used only two oscillators + two waveshapers for the (0,1) partials; separate VCAs/contours for the two partials; pitch contour judged unnecessary by author. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/practical-snare-drum-synthesis
- Snare and clap share the noise source (phasing when played together). https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_TR-909

## LT / MT / HT (toms)
- Owner's manual: Level, Tune (clockwise higher pitch; usable as melodic tom), Decay (fully CCW mutes). https://archive.org/download/synthmanual-roland-tr-909-owners-manual/rolandtr-909ownersmanual_djvu.txt
- Same circuit for all three, only VCO main tunings differ. Mid tom: three VCOs (same design as snare VCO) with different charging capacitors C32, C33, C34; VCO-3 highest frequency, VCO-2 lowest. http://www.network-909.de/toms.htm
- On TRIG: ENV-4 starts a pitch modulation of all VCOs through the Control Voltage Generator; Tune (VR13) adjusts the control voltage manually; Q30-32 discharge C32-34 to reset/sync all VCOs. http://www.network-909.de/toms.htm
- Each VCO -> diode clipper -> sine-ish. VCO-1's clipper is TRIG-controlled (C38, Q23): clipping slowly goes from hard (square-ish) to soft (sine) after the hit. http://www.network-909.de/toms.htm
- Each VCO has its own VCA and envelope; only ENV-2 (VCO-2) has an adjustable Decay (VR17) so with long Decay only one oscillator remains after some ms. VCO-3 is mixed with "Tom noise" (modified common noise) before its VCA for a natural attack. http://www.network-909.de/toms.htm
- Tom sounds were designed from acoustic-tom analysis with an in-house A/D converter. https://www.attackmagazine.com/features/interview/atsushi-hoshiai-the-man-behind-the-tr-909/

## RS (rim shot)
- Three bridged-T networks (band-pass, ringing) triggered by a positive pulse from D89; diode clipper D91/D92 on the mix; VCA driven by IC48b/Q64 and simple envelope C119; final high-pass removes low frequencies. http://www.network-909.de/rimshot.htm
- Frequencies stated: f1=500 Hz, f2=220 Hz, f3=1000 Hz; decay set by network Q: Q = sqrt(R1/R2) / ( sqrt(C1/C2)+sqrt(C2/C1) ). Component tolerance moves the frequencies machine to machine. http://www.network-909.de/rimshot.htm
- Only a Level knob (owner's manual). Its trigger also drives the TRIG OUT jack. https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt

## CP (hand clap)
- Same circuit concept as the TR-808: common noise -> band-pass (IC26b and RCs) split into two parts. http://www.network-909.de/handclap.htm , https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- Part 1 "claps": VCA (IC30; parts list shows BA662A vari-conductance amp) under a special envelope of four attack-decay envelopes (op-amps a-d), each finishing triggers the next ("clapping of several hands"). Whittle: VCA driven by a series of short pulses "like a few cycles of a sawtooth wave", pulse density set by trimpot TM through dual PNP Q38 (log-to-linear). http://www.network-909.de/handclap.htm , https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- Part 2 "pseudo-reverb": second VCA (Q37) with simple envelope (C61 in it); longer, softer, slower decay. Mixed at IC28a with level control. http://www.network-909.de/handclap.htm , https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- Noise input level into clap VCA via R201 (1k); R208 (47k) feeds the filter section. https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- Level only (owner's manual). No ms values were found in any fetched text.

## CH / OH (hi-hats, 6-bit sample)
- Samples: 6-bit PCM in mask ROM; hi-hat sampled by Hoshiai from a 14" Paiste Sound Edge top and 14" A. Zildjian New Beat bottom; data amplitude-processed on a computer after digitization, then stored. https://www.attackmagazine.com/features/interview/atsushi-hoshiai-the-man-behind-the-tr-909/ , http://www.network-909.de/hihatand.htm
- Samples were compressed before 6-bit quantization for better S/N, so the raw replay envelope differs from the real hat; a VCA restores the envelope. https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt
- ROM parts list: HN61256P-PC42 Crash, -PC43 Hi-hat, -PC44 Ride (CMOS mask ROMs). https://archive.org/download/manualzz-id-1060785/1060785_djvu.txt
- Playback: TRIG resets address counters IC70/IC71 to 0 -> IC72a "run"; 2-NAND-gate oscillator (no crystal) about 60 kHz, divided by two (IC73 flip-flop) = clock for address counters; ROM IC69 -> latch IC68 -> resistor-network DAC (RA9). Run stops when counter reaches an end address. https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt , http://www.network-909.de/hihatand.htm
- Closed vs Open share one ROM; CLOSED/OPEN level gated into diode ORs (D196-199) selects different start/end addresses (OCR of the address table is garbled; digits seen: 110 0000 0000 0000, 010 0000 0000 0000, 000 0000 0000 0000, 111 1111 1111 1111; assignment to modes not recoverable from OCR). https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt
- Amplitude envelope: analog decay capacitor C135 drives an anti-log converter -> VCA. CH decay: Q73 charges C135 through R451 + VR21 (CH Decay); OH decay: Q73 off, charges through R452 + VR23 (OH Decay). The CH path resistance is about 1/10 that of the OH path. Turning VR23 slightly shifts CH decay. http://www.network-909.de/hihatand.htm , https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt
- Output: two cascaded low-pass filters after the VCA remove DAC/aliasing HF, then level amp. (SOS: one LPF between VCA and output to remove quantisation noise and aliasing.) http://www.network-909.de/hihatand.htm , https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/practical-cymbal-synthesis
- Owner's manual: single Decay knob listed for hi-hat (CCW mutes); no Tune knob on the stock machine. Whittle's mod adds one: changes oscillator (sample playback speed) from about 0.43 to about 1.7 of original; oscillator R492 = 12k, C160 = 470 pF in original; stops oscillating if pushed too high. https://archive.org/download/synthmanual-roland-tr-909-owners-manual/rolandtr-909ownersmanual_djvu.txt , https://www.firstpr.com.au/rwi/tr-909/TR-909-Sound-Mods.pdf
- Hi-hat accent: Accent B can apply to Closed HH only (sequencer). https://archive.org/download/synthmanual-roland-tr-909-owners-manual/rolandtr-909ownersmanual_djvu.txt

## Crash / Ride
- Same digital architecture as the hats (sample ROM, resistor DAC, VCA); both sampled from one 18" Paiste Thin Crash: crash struck on the edge, ride on the bow. https://www.attackmagazine.com/features/interview/atsushi-hoshiai-the-man-behind-the-tr-909/ , https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt
- No decay knob. Envelope restoration uses the ROM addresses themselves: address counter -> second DAC (RA11) -> anti-log (IC52b, Q70) -> VCA Q71 ("voltage controlled potentiometer") giving the CRASH decay curve. Done so decay scales when pitch (clock) changes. https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt , http://www.network-909.de/hihatand.htm
- Owner's manual: knob is Tone, "adjusts the pitch of the Cymbal sound" (plus Level). In circuit it is the clock-oscillator speed ("30 kHz oscillator" in SOS; about 60 kHz /2 in service notes). https://archive.org/download/synthmanual-roland-tr-909-owners-manual/rolandtr-909ownersmanual_djvu.txt , https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/practical-cymbal-synthesis
- SOS: signal chain = 30 kHz clock -> address counter -> six-bit data table -> DAC -> VCA -> low-pass -> out; second counter output -> DAC -> anti-log -> VCA control. https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/practical-cymbal-synthesis
- Crash and ride each have their own Level and ROM (IC62 for crash named in text). https://archive.org/download/synth_Roland_TR-909_service_notes/Roland_TR-909_service_notes_djvu.txt

## Gaps and disagreements
- BD oscillator waveform: SOS says sawtooth into a waveshaper (https://www.soundonsound.com/techniques/practical-bass-drum-synthesis); network-909 (analysis of service-manual schematic), Whittle, and an Elektronauts post say triangle VCO + diode clipper (same circuit family as the snare, whose service notes say triangle). Prefer triangle+diode clipper.
- BD Tune: owner's manual = pitch; network-909 = pitch-modulation depth (VR2); Whittle = decay time of the downward pitch sweep (base frequency not user-controlled). Unresolved without reading the schematic image.
- Noise shift register size: service notes OCR = two registers cascaded for "32 stages"; Whittle = "two 18 stage"; parts list = HD14006BP "18-bit static shift register". 2 x 18 = 36 vs 32; clock frequency of the noise generator not found.
- SD ENV5 / accent: service notes say ACCENT gated via Q41 into ENV5 (HF noise amount); Whittle says ENV5 is the same irrespective of accent and ENV4 carries accent (via D60). Disagree.
- Snare Tone: owner's manual "brighter"; circuit evidence (Whittle) says it is the decay of the main snappy noise envelope (VR7). Both can be true; not confirmed in service-note prose.
- SOS snare article quotes 180 Hz and 330 Hz partials: these belong to the author's generic Nord patch, NOT measured from the 909. No fetched source gives the 909 snare VCO frequencies. A ModWiggler thread (t=222069, "TR-909 Tom and Snare oscillator frequencies") and a Gearspace thread returned 403 to curl; only search-snippet content (low tom ratio VCO3:VCO1:VCO2 = 1:1.5:2.77) exists, unverified, not quoted as fact.
- NOT FOUND in any fetched text: Hz of BD/SD/tom VCOs; any envelope time in ms except snare pitch bend about 20 ms, ENV4 flat about 24 ms, trigger 2 ms, rim trig out 20 ms; BD pitch sweep amount/time; clap envelope times/count timings (four stages, no ms); rim-shot envelope time; noise filter cutoffs; hat/ride/crash sample lengths, sample rate other than about 30 kHz at nominal tune; crash/ride filter cutoffs; Total Accent knob circuit scaling; accent depth in dB.
- Component values (R/C) are only partial: schematics are scan images (service-manual PDFs have no text layer); I did not read pages as images. A real schematic read is needed for exact RC time constants. Whittle's PDF appends BD/SD/HCP/HH schematic crops as images too.
- Hi-hat: owner's manual lists one Decay heading; circuit has two pots (VR21 CH, VR23 OH). Panel actually has separate CH and OH decay knobs (inferred from circuit; manual OCR groups them).
- Address-table bits for open/closed hat unreadable from OCR (above).
- Not relevant / ruled out: https://speech.di.uoa.gr/ICMC-SMC-2014/images/VOL_2/1453.pdf is the TR-808 cymbal (analog, six square oscillators), NOT the 909; ghostintranslation/ds909 is "not quite a clone" Teensy synth, no circuit data; Steda SR-909 pages are product news only (no circuit); synthmanuals.com returned HTTP 500, manualslib 403; Electric Druid, NeinOhNein snare page and a DAFx 909 paper were not found (web search returned none).
- Secondary summaries (Wikipedia, SOS review) only restate the sampled-hats / shared-noise facts; used sparingly.
